# Food Poisoning

## Common symptoms
- Nausea and vomiting
- Diarrhea, sometimes watery
- Abdominal cramps
- Mild fever
- Weakness or fatigue
- Symptoms often starting within hours of eating a particular food

## Ask the patient about
- What was eaten recently, and did others who ate the same food also fall ill?
- When did symptoms start after eating, and how many episodes of vomiting/diarrhea so far?
- Is there blood in vomit or stool?
- Is the person able to keep any fluids down?
- Any fever, severe abdominal pain, or muscle weakness?
- How is urination — normal, reduced, or dark colored?

## Red flags — seek urgent/emergency care immediately
- Signs of dehydration: little or no urine, dry mouth, dizziness, sunken eyes
- Blood in vomit or stool
- High fever or severe abdominal pain
- Persistent vomiting with inability to keep any fluids down
- Muscle weakness, blurred vision, or difficulty swallowing/breathing (possible serious toxin exposure)
- Symptoms in an infant, elderly person, or pregnant woman not improving

## Plain-language guidance
Rest and sip small amounts of clean water or oral rehydration solution frequently to replace lost fluids, resuming light, bland food gradually as tolerated once vomiting settles. If dehydration signs appear, fluids cannot be kept down, or there is blood in vomit/stool or high fever, seek clinic care promptly. A doctor should review the full picture, especially if symptoms are severe or affect a vulnerable person.
