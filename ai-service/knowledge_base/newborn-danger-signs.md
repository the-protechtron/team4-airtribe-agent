# Newborn Danger Signs

## Common symptoms (normal newborn behaviors, for context)
- Frequent feeding and sleeping
- Occasional mild fussiness
- Soft, irregular breathing pattern
- Mild jaundice appearing after day 2-3 that resolves on its own in many cases

## Ask the patient about
- How old is the baby (in days), and how is feeding going (frequency, strength of suck)?
- Any fever, or does the baby feel unusually cold to touch?
- Is the baby unusually drowsy, difficult to wake, or unusually irritable?
- Any yellowing of the skin or eyes, and how soon after birth did it appear?
- Any difficulty breathing, grunting, or bluish color noticed?
- Any redness, pus, or foul smell around the umbilical cord?

## Red flags — seek urgent/emergency care immediately
- Fever or low body temperature (baby feels hot or unusually cold)
- Difficulty breathing, grunting, or bluish lips/skin
- Poor or no feeding, or persistent vomiting
- Lethargy, difficulty waking, or floppy body
- Convulsions or unusual jerking movements
- Yellowing of skin/eyes appearing within the first day of life, or spreading/worsening jaundice
- Redness, swelling, or discharge from the umbilical cord area

## Plain-language guidance
Newborns can become seriously unwell very quickly, so any fever, breathing difficulty, poor feeding, unusual drowsiness, or jaundice appearing early should be treated as an emergency and taken to a clinic or hospital immediately, not observed at home. Keep the baby warm, continue attempting feeds, and avoid delay if any danger sign appears. A doctor should examine the baby directly, as newborn illness can progress faster than in older children or adults.
