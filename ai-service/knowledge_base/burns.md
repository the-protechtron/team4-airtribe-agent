# Burns

## Common symptoms
- Redness and pain at the burn site (minor/superficial burns)
- Blistering (moderate burns)
- White, leathery, or charred skin with little pain (severe/deep burns, nerve damage)
- Swelling around the burned area
- Broken or peeling skin

## Ask the patient about
- What caused the burn (heat, fire, chemical, electrical, hot liquid), and when did it happen?
- How large is the burned area, and roughly what part of the body (relative to hand-size comparisons)?
- Is there blistering, or does the skin look white/charred with reduced pain?
- Was the burn caused by a chemical or electrical source, which need different handling?
- Any signs of infection developing (pus, increasing redness, foul smell) if the burn is not new?
- Any difficulty breathing if the face, neck, or airway area was involved?

## Red flags — seek urgent/emergency care immediately
- Large burns (larger than the size of the patient's palm) or burns on face, hands, feet, groin, or major joints
- Deep burns with white, charred, or leathery skin
- Chemical or electrical burns
- Burns with difficulty breathing or suspected airway involvement
- Signs of infection: spreading redness, pus, fever
- Burns in infants, young children, or elderly patients

## Plain-language guidance
Cool a fresh burn under clean running water for several minutes (not ice), gently remove tight items like jewelry near the area, and cover loosely with a clean, non-fluffy cloth — do not apply toothpaste, oil, or other home remedies. Small, minor burns may be manageable with basic first aid, but anything large, deep, on sensitive areas, or from chemicals/electricity needs prompt clinic or hospital care. A doctor should review the full picture, especially for burns beyond minor size.
