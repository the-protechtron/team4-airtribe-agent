# Urinary Tract Infection (UTI)

## Common symptoms
- Burning or pain during urination
- Frequent urge to urinate, often passing small amounts
- Cloudy, strong-smelling, or blood-tinged urine
- Lower abdominal or pelvic discomfort
- Mild fever (in some cases)
- In children or elderly: unusual fussiness, confusion, or general unwellness without clear urinary complaints

## Ask the patient about
- How long has the burning or frequent urination been present?
- Any blood or unusual color/smell in the urine?
- Any fever, back pain, or flank pain (possible kidney involvement)?
- Any prior UTIs, and how were they treated?
- Is the person pregnant, diabetic, or have any condition that raises infection risk?
- Fluid intake habits and any recent dehydration?

## Red flags — seek urgent/emergency care immediately
- Fever with chills alongside urinary symptoms (possible kidney infection)
- Back or flank pain with fever and vomiting
- Blood in urine that is significant or persistent
- Confusion, especially in elderly patients
- Symptoms in a pregnant woman
- Inability to urinate at all despite the urge

## Plain-language guidance
Drinking plenty of clean water and urinating regularly (not holding it in) can help ease mild symptoms, but a burning sensation with frequent urination usually needs a urine test and proper treatment from a clinic to fully clear the infection. If fever, back pain, or blood in urine develop, this could mean the infection has spread and needs prompt attention. A doctor should review the full picture, especially for repeated or worsening infections.
