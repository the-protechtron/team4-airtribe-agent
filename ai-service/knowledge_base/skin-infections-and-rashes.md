# Skin Infections and Rashes

## Common symptoms
- Redness, itching, or irritation of the skin
- Bumps, blisters, or scaling patches
- Pus-filled spots or boils
- Warmth and swelling around an affected area
- Spreading discoloration or streaks from a wound site

## Ask the patient about
- When did the rash or skin change start, and has it spread since then?
- Is there itching, pain, warmth, or discharge from the area?
- Any recent insect bites, new soap/products, plant contact, or exposure to someone with a similar rash?
- Any fever alongside the skin changes?
- Any known allergies or previous similar skin problems?
- Is the affected area near a wound or cut that may have gotten infected?

## Red flags — seek urgent/emergency care immediately
- Rapidly spreading redness, swelling, or red streaks (possible serious infection)
- Fever with a spreading or worsening rash
- Widespread blistering or skin peeling
- Signs of an allergic reaction alongside the rash (swelling of face/lips, difficulty breathing)
- Pus with increasing pain, warmth, and swelling (possible abscess)
- Rash with joint pain, severe headache, or the person appearing very unwell

## Plain-language guidance
Keep the affected skin clean and dry, avoid scratching or applying unknown home remedies, and watch for spreading redness, fever, or worsening pain, which suggest the infection needs clinic treatment. Mild, localized, non-spreading rashes may settle with basic hygiene, but anything spreading quickly, very painful, or accompanied by fever should be checked soon. A doctor should review the full picture to identify the cause and appropriate care.
