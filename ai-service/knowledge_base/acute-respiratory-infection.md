# Acute Respiratory Infection

## Common symptoms
- Cough (dry or with mucus)
- Runny or blocked nose
- Sore throat
- Mild fever
- Sneezing and general body discomfort
- Mild fatigue

## Ask the patient about
- How long has the cough or congestion lasted?
- Is there fever, and if so how high does it feel?
- Any shortness of breath, wheezing, or chest tightness?
- Is the cough producing mucus, and what color is it?
- Any known exposure to others with similar illness?
- Any underlying lung conditions, smoking history, or existing breathing problems?

## Red flags — seek urgent/emergency care immediately
- Difficulty breathing, rapid breathing, or breathlessness at rest
- Bluish lips or face
- Chest pain with breathing
- High fever that doesn't improve after a few days
- Confusion or extreme drowsiness
- Symptoms significantly worse in a young child, elderly person, or someone with existing heart/lung disease

## Plain-language guidance
Most upper respiratory infections settle with rest, fluids, warm liquids, and time, and steam inhalation or a humid environment may ease congestion. If breathing becomes difficult, fever persists beyond a few days, or the person is very young, elderly, or has an existing lung condition, arrange a clinic visit rather than waiting. A doctor should review the full picture, especially if symptoms do not improve as expected.
