# Chest Pain (Cardiac Red Flags)

## Common symptoms
- Pain, pressure, or tightness in the chest
- Pain that may spread to the arm, jaw, neck, or back
- Shortness of breath
- Sweating or feeling clammy
- Nausea or lightheadedness
- Fatigue, especially with exertion

## Ask the patient about
- Where exactly is the pain, and does it spread anywhere else (arm, jaw, back)?
- Is the pain constant, or does it come with activity and ease with rest?
- Any shortness of breath, sweating, nausea, or dizziness with the pain?
- How long has the pain lasted, and how severe is it (mild ache vs. crushing pressure)?
- Any history of heart disease, high blood pressure, diabetes, or smoking?
- Any recent injury, cough, or fever that could explain the pain differently?

## Red flags — seek urgent/emergency care immediately
- Crushing, squeezing, or pressure-like chest pain, especially lasting more than a few minutes
- Pain spreading to the arm, jaw, neck, or back
- Shortness of breath, sweating, or nausea alongside chest pain
- Sudden weakness, dizziness, or fainting
- Pain in someone with known heart disease, diabetes, high blood pressure, or older age
- Any chest pain that feels different or more severe than usual for the person

## Plain-language guidance
Any chest pain that is severe, persistent, spreading to the arm/jaw, or accompanied by breathlessness, sweating, or nausea should be treated as a possible heart emergency — get to the nearest hospital immediately rather than waiting to see if it passes. Keep the person calm, resting, and seated or lying comfortably while arranging urgent transport. A doctor must evaluate any significant chest pain promptly, as timing matters greatly in cardiac emergencies.
