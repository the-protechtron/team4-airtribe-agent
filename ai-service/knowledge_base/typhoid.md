# Typhoid Fever

## Common symptoms
- Prolonged fever that gradually rises over days
- Severe headache and general weakness
- Abdominal pain or discomfort
- Loss of appetite
- Constipation or diarrhea
- Rose-colored small spots on the skin (less common)

## Ask the patient about
- How many days has the fever lasted, and has it been rising gradually?
- Any abdominal pain, bloating, or changes in bowel habits?
- Has the patient eaten or drunk from unsafe water or food sources recently?
- Any nausea, vomiting, or noticeable weakness?
- Has anyone nearby had similar symptoms recently?
- Any prior typhoid illness or vaccination?

## Red flags — seek urgent/emergency care immediately
- Severe abdominal pain, especially with a rigid or very tender belly
- Blood in stool or vomit
- High persistent fever beyond a week without improvement
- Confusion, delirium, or extreme lethargy
- Signs of dehydration (very little urine, dry mouth, sunken eyes)
- Sudden worsening of pain, which could suggest intestinal complications

## Plain-language guidance
Typhoid needs proper testing and confirmed treatment from a clinic, so someone with a persistent, slowly rising fever with stomach pain and weakness should be evaluated soon rather than waiting it out at home. Encourage safe drinking water, light food, and rest in the meantime, and seek care urgently if severe abdominal pain or bleeding develops. A doctor should review the full picture before any treatment is decided.
