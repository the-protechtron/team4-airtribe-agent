# Dengue Fever

## Common symptoms
- Sudden high fever
- Severe headache, especially behind the eyes
- Muscle, joint, and bone pain ("breakbone" pain)
- Skin rash appearing a few days after fever starts
- Mild bleeding such as gum or nose bleeding
- Nausea and vomiting

## Ask the patient about
- When did the fever start, and has it suddenly become high?
- Any pain behind the eyes, severe joint or muscle pain, or rash?
- Any bleeding from gums, nose, or noticing blood in stool/vomit?
- Any recent mosquito exposure or others nearby with similar symptoms?
- Any abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, or reduced urination?
- Has the fever suddenly dropped while the patient feels worse, not better?

## Red flags — seek urgent/emergency care immediately
- Severe abdominal pain or persistent vomiting
- Bleeding from gums, nose, or blood in vomit/stool
- Cold, clammy skin or restlessness/irritability
- Difficulty breathing or rapid breathing
- Fever suddenly drops but the person becomes weaker, drowsy, or confused
- Little or no urine output

## Plain-language guidance
Dengue can worsen suddenly a few days after the fever begins, so anyone with high fever plus severe body aches, rash, or bleeding signs should be checked at a clinic promptly, especially around days 3-7 of illness. Encourage fluids and rest, and avoid unnecessary strain; watch closely for warning signs even as the fever seems to improve. A doctor should review the full picture, as monitoring often needs to continue for several days.
