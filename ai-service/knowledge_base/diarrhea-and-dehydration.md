# Diarrhea and Dehydration

## Common symptoms
- Frequent loose or watery stools
- Abdominal cramping
- Nausea or vomiting
- Thirst, dry mouth, or reduced urination
- Tiredness or dizziness, especially on standing
- In children: fewer wet diapers, sunken eyes, lack of tears when crying

## Ask the patient about
- How many episodes of loose stool per day, and for how many days?
- Is there blood or mucus in the stool?
- Is the patient (or child) able to drink and keep fluids down?
- How much and how often is the patient urinating?
- Any fever, vomiting, or severe abdominal pain alongside the diarrhea?
- For children: are they alert and playful, or unusually drowsy and irritable?

## Red flags — seek urgent/emergency care immediately
- Signs of severe dehydration: sunken eyes, very dry mouth, little or no urine, extreme lethargy
- Blood in stool or high fever with diarrhea
- Diarrhea in an infant, elderly person, or pregnant woman that isn't improving
- Persistent vomiting preventing any fluid intake
- Confusion, fainting, or very rapid heartbeat
- Diarrhea lasting more than a few days without improvement

## Plain-language guidance
The priority is replacing lost fluids and salts steadily with small, frequent sips of clean water or oral rehydration solutions, and continuing light, easily digestible food as tolerated. If the person (especially a child or elderly adult) cannot keep fluids down, shows signs of dehydration, or has blood in the stool, get to a clinic quickly. A doctor should review the full picture to guide further care.
