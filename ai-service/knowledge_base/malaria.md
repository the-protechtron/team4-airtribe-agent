# Malaria

## Common symptoms
- Fever that may come in cycles with chills and sweating
- Severe headache and body aches
- Nausea, vomiting, or diarrhea
- Fatigue and weakness
- Sweating heavily as fever breaks

## Ask the patient about
- Does the fever come and go in cycles (e.g., every 1-3 days) with shaking chills?
- Has the patient been in or traveled to an area known for mosquito-borne illness recently?
- Any recent mosquito bites or lack of bed net use?
- Any history of malaria before, and was it treated fully?
- Any yellowing of eyes/skin, dark urine, or reduced urination?
- Is the patient pregnant or a young child (higher risk groups)?

## Red flags — seek urgent/emergency care immediately
- Confusion, seizures, or loss of consciousness
- Yellowing of skin or eyes (jaundice)
- Very dark or reduced urine output
- Difficulty breathing or rapid breathing
- Repeated vomiting with inability to keep fluids down
- Fever in a pregnant woman or young child not improving quickly

## Plain-language guidance
In areas where malaria is common, any cyclical fever with chills should be tested and treated quickly at a clinic, since malaria can worsen rapidly if delayed. Encourage fluids and rest while arranging prompt testing; do not wait at home if fever is high or red-flag signs appear. A doctor should review the full picture and confirm diagnosis with proper testing before treatment.
