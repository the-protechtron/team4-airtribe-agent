# Eye and Ear Infections

## Common symptoms
- Eye: redness, itching, discharge, watering, or a gritty feeling
- Eye: sensitivity to light or blurred vision
- Ear: pain, itching, or a feeling of fullness/blockage
- Ear: discharge or reduced hearing
- Ear: in children, tugging at the ear or unusual irritability

## Ask the patient about
- Which eye or ear is affected, and how long have symptoms been present?
- Is there discharge, and what color/consistency (watery, pus-like)?
- Any pain, itching, or sensitivity to light or sound?
- Any recent cold, water exposure (swimming), injury, or foreign object contact?
- Any fever alongside the eye or ear symptoms?
- In a child: is there ear-tugging, poor sleep, or persistent crying?

## Red flags — seek urgent/emergency care immediately
- Sudden vision loss or severe eye pain
- Eye injury involving a chemical, sharp object, or significant trauma
- Severe swelling around the eye with fever (possible spreading infection)
- Ear pain with high fever, severe headache, or swelling behind the ear
- Sudden hearing loss
- Discharge of blood or clear fluid from the ear after a head injury

## Plain-language guidance
Mild eye or ear discomfort with clear discharge and no fever may improve with gentle cleaning and avoiding irritants, but persistent redness, pain, or pus-like discharge usually needs a clinic visit for proper examination and care. Avoid inserting objects into the ear or using unknown eye drops without guidance. A doctor should review the full picture, especially for vision changes, severe pain, or symptoms in a young child.
