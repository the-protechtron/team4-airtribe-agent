# Stroke Warning Signs (FAST Criteria)

## Common symptoms
- Sudden weakness or numbness on one side of the face, arm, or leg
- Sudden difficulty speaking or slurred speech
- Sudden confusion or trouble understanding others
- Sudden vision problems in one or both eyes
- Sudden severe headache with no known cause
- Sudden difficulty walking, dizziness, or loss of balance

## Ask the patient about
- Using the FAST check: does the Face droop on one side when smiling? Can both Arms be raised evenly, or does one drift down? Is Speech slurred or hard to understand?
- Exactly what Time did symptoms start (critical for treatment decisions)?
- Is there sudden numbness or weakness on one side of the body?
- Any sudden vision loss, severe headache, confusion, or loss of balance?
- Any history of high blood pressure, diabetes, heart disease, or previous stroke?
- Has anything like this happened before and resolved on its own?

## Red flags — seek urgent/emergency care immediately
- Any FAST sign: Face drooping, Arm weakness, or Speech difficulty — Time to call for emergency transport
- Sudden numbness or weakness on one side of the body
- Sudden severe headache unlike any before
- Sudden vision loss or confusion
- Sudden loss of balance, coordination, or consciousness
- Symptoms that come and go should still be treated as urgent, as they can precede a larger stroke

## Plain-language guidance
If any FAST sign appears — Face drooping, Arm weakness, or Speech difficulty — treat it as a time-critical emergency and get the person to the nearest hospital immediately, noting the exact time symptoms started since this affects treatment options. Do not give food, drink, or medicine by mouth while arranging transport, and keep the person safe and calm. A doctor must evaluate any suspected stroke as fast as possible, as early treatment greatly improves outcomes.
