# Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)

## Common symptoms
- Often no symptoms at all (silent condition)
- Occasional headache
- Dizziness
- Blurred vision
- Nosebleeds (uncommon, usually only with very high readings)
- Fatigue

## Ask the patient about
- Has blood pressure ever been measured or diagnosed as high before?
- Is the person on any regular treatment, and are they taking it consistently?
- Any headaches, dizziness, blurred vision, or chest discomfort?
- Family history of high blood pressure, heart disease, or stroke?
- Diet habits (salt intake), activity level, and stress levels?
- Any swelling in legs/feet or shortness of breath noticed?

## Red flags — seek urgent/emergency care immediately
- Severe headache with confusion, blurred vision, or difficulty speaking
- Chest pain or shortness of breath
- Sudden weakness or numbness on one side of the body
- Very high measured blood pressure with symptoms (not just a number alone)
- Nosebleed that won't stop alongside severe headache
- Seizures or loss of consciousness

## Plain-language guidance
Because high blood pressure often has no obvious symptoms, regular checking and follow-up matter more than waiting for warning signs, along with reducing salt intake, staying active, and managing stress where possible. If severe headache, chest pain, vision changes, or weakness occur, this could signal a dangerous spike and needs urgent care. A doctor should review blood pressure readings over time and the full picture before adjusting any treatment.
