# Asthma / Breathing Difficulty

## Common symptoms
- Wheezing or whistling sound while breathing
- Shortness of breath
- Chest tightness
- Cough, often worse at night or with activity
- Difficulty speaking full sentences during an episode

## Ask the patient about
- How long has the breathing difficulty lasted, and does it come and go?
- Is there a known history of asthma or allergies?
- Any known triggers such as dust, smoke, cold air, or exercise?
- Is the person able to speak normally, or are they struggling to talk?
- Any blue tinge to lips or fingertips noticed?
- Has this happened before, and what usually helps?

## Red flags — seek urgent/emergency care immediately
- Severe difficulty breathing or inability to speak full sentences
- Bluish lips, tongue, or fingertips
- Chest or neck muscles pulling in with each breath
- Drowsiness, confusion, or exhaustion from effort to breathe
- No improvement despite usual measures
- Silent chest (no wheeze heard despite obvious struggle to breathe) — a serious sign

## Plain-language guidance
Help the person sit upright in a calm, well-ventilated space, away from known triggers like smoke or dust, and avoid exertion during an episode. If breathing does not ease quickly, or if speaking, walking, or lying flat becomes difficult, seek urgent medical care rather than waiting at home. A doctor should review the full picture, especially if this is a first episode or symptoms are worsening.
