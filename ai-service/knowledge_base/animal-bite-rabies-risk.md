# Animal Bite / Rabies Risk

## Common symptoms
- Bite or scratch wound, possibly with bleeding
- Pain, redness, or swelling around the wound
- Later (if infected with rabies, after an incubation period): fever, headache, tingling near the wound
- Anxiety, agitation, or unusual behavior (later rabies symptom)
- Difficulty swallowing or fear of water (later rabies symptom)

## Ask the patient about
- What animal caused the bite or scratch (dog, cat, monkey, bat, wild animal)?
- Was the animal a stray, and is its vaccination status known?
- Was the wound washed with soap and water already?
- How deep or severe is the wound, and where on the body is it?
- Has the person received rabies vaccination before, or a tetanus shot recently?
- Any unusual behavior noted in the animal (aggression, foaming, unprovoked attack)?

## Red flags — seek urgent/emergency care immediately
- Bite from a stray, wild, or unknown-vaccination-status animal
- Bites to the face, neck, hands, or multiple deep wounds
- Any bite from a bat, or an animal behaving strangely
- Signs of wound infection: spreading redness, pus, fever
- Any neurological symptoms appearing after a bite (tingling, agitation, difficulty swallowing) — this is a medical emergency
- Heavy bleeding that won't stop

## Plain-language guidance
Wash the bite or scratch immediately and thoroughly with soap and running water for several minutes, then go to a clinic promptly — rabies risk assessment and vaccination (if needed) is time-sensitive and should not be delayed regardless of how minor the wound looks. Cover the wound loosely afterward and avoid home remedies. A doctor should assess the wound, the animal's risk status, and the full picture to decide on rabies and tetanus prevention.
