# Pneumonia Warning Signs

## Common symptoms
- Cough with thick or discolored mucus
- Fever, sometimes with chills
- Chest pain that worsens with breathing or coughing
- Fast or labored breathing
- Fatigue and general weakness
- Reduced appetite, especially in children and elderly

## Ask the patient about
- How long has the cough and fever lasted, and is breathing faster than usual?
- Any chest pain, and does it worsen when breathing deeply or coughing?
- Is there noticeable effort to breathe, such as flaring nostrils or chest pulling in (especially in children)?
- Any bluish tint to lips or fingertips noticed?
- Any underlying conditions like heart disease, diabetes, or being very young/elderly?
- Has appetite or activity level dropped noticeably?

## Red flags — seek urgent/emergency care immediately
- Fast, labored, or grunting breathing
- Chest indrawing (skin pulling in between ribs) in a child
- Bluish lips, tongue, or fingertips
- Confusion, extreme drowsiness, or unresponsiveness
- Inability to drink or feed (in infants)
- High fever with chills not improving, or worsening chest pain

## Plain-language guidance
Pneumonia can become serious quickly, especially in young children, elderly people, and those with weak immunity, so noticeable fast or labored breathing alongside fever and cough should not be managed at home. Keep the person resting, hydrated, and comfortable while arranging urgent clinic or hospital evaluation if any red-flag breathing signs appear. A doctor should review the full picture, including listening to the chest, before deciding on treatment.
