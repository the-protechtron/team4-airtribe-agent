# Fractures and Sprains

## Common symptoms
- Pain at the injury site, worsened by movement or weight-bearing
- Swelling and bruising
- Reduced range of motion
- Deformity or unusual angle of a limb (suggests fracture)
- Tenderness to touch

## Ask the patient about
- How did the injury happen (fall, twist, direct blow, accident)?
- Can the limb or joint be moved or bear weight at all?
- Is there visible deformity, or does the limb look out of its normal shape?
- Where exactly is the pain, and how severe is it?
- Any numbness, tingling, or loss of sensation beyond the injury site?
- Any open wound near the injury, or bone visible?

## Red flags — seek urgent/emergency care immediately
- Visible deformity or bone protruding through the skin
- Inability to move or bear any weight on the limb
- Numbness, tingling, or loss of pulse/color beyond the injury (possible circulation or nerve damage)
- Severe swelling that develops rapidly
- Head, neck, or spine injury suspected
- Severe pain not easing with rest and support

## Plain-language guidance
For a suspected sprain or minor injury, rest the area, apply a cold compress, and support/elevate the limb while avoiding weight-bearing until it can be assessed. If there is visible deformity, an open wound, numbness, or inability to move or bear weight at all, avoid moving the area further and get to a clinic for an X-ray and proper support. A doctor should review the full picture to distinguish a sprain from a fracture and guide recovery.
