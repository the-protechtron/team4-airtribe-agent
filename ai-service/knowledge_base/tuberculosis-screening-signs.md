# Tuberculosis Screening Signs

## Common symptoms
- Cough lasting more than two to three weeks
- Coughing up blood or blood-streaked mucus
- Unexplained weight loss
- Night sweats
- Ongoing low-grade fever
- Persistent fatigue and loss of appetite

## Ask the patient about
- How long has the cough lasted, and has it been more than 2-3 weeks?
- Any blood in the mucus or sputum when coughing?
- Has there been unexplained weight loss recently?
- Any night sweats or recurring low-grade fever?
- Any known contact with someone diagnosed with tuberculosis?
- Any history of TB treatment before, and was it completed fully?

## Red flags — seek urgent/emergency care immediately
- Coughing up significant amounts of blood
- Severe difficulty breathing or chest pain
- Marked weight loss with extreme weakness
- High fever with confusion
- Known TB contact with worsening symptoms
- Symptoms in someone with HIV or another condition weakening immunity

## Plain-language guidance
A cough lasting more than two to three weeks, especially with weight loss, night sweats, or blood in the mucus, should be evaluated at a clinic for TB testing, since early diagnosis and full treatment matter a great deal. Encourage the person to cover coughs, ventilate living spaces, and avoid close contact with infants until evaluated. A doctor should review the full picture and arrange proper testing before any treatment begins.
