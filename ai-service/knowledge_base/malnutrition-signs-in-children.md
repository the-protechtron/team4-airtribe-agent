# Malnutrition Signs in Children

## Common symptoms
- Low weight or height for age
- Visible thinning of arms, legs, or ribs
- Swelling of feet, hands, or face (in severe cases)
- Frequent infections or slow recovery from illness
- Low energy, irritability, or reduced interest in surroundings
- Delayed growth or developmental milestones

## Ask the patient about
- What has the child been eating, and how often each day?
- Has the child lost weight or failed to gain weight recently?
- Any recent illness, diarrhea, or infections affecting appetite?
- Any swelling of the feet, hands, or face noticed?
- Is the child reaching expected developmental milestones (sitting, walking, talking)?
- What is the family's access to food and clean water like?

## Red flags — seek urgent/emergency care immediately
- Visible severe wasting (very thin arms/legs, ribs showing)
- Swelling of the feet, hands, or face
- Extreme lethargy or unresponsiveness
- Persistent vomiting or refusal to eat/drink
- Signs of dehydration alongside poor nutrition
- Very low body temperature or difficulty staying warm

## Plain-language guidance
A child who is not gaining weight, appears very thin, or shows swelling should be checked at a clinic soon, since malnutrition affects growth, immunity, and development if untreated. Encourage regular, nutrient-rich meals appropriate for the child's age, and continue feeding through illness where possible rather than restricting food. A doctor or health worker should review the child's growth and full picture to guide any nutritional support.
