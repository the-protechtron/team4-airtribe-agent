# Allergic Reactions

## Common symptoms
- Skin itching, hives, or raised red welts
- Sneezing, runny nose, or itchy/watery eyes
- Mild swelling of lips, face, or eyelids
- Nausea or stomach discomfort (with food-related reactions)
- Wheezing or throat tightness (more severe reaction)

## Ask the patient about
- What was the person exposed to (food, insect sting, medicine, plant, new product)?
- How soon after exposure did symptoms start?
- Is there any swelling of the face, lips, tongue, or throat?
- Any difficulty breathing, wheezing, or hoarseness?
- Has this happened before, and how severe was it previously?
- Any known allergies already identified?

## Red flags — seek urgent/emergency care immediately
- Swelling of the lips, tongue, throat, or face
- Difficulty breathing, wheezing, or a feeling of throat tightness
- Dizziness, fainting, or a sudden drop in alertness
- Rapidly spreading hives with breathing difficulty (possible anaphylaxis)
- Vomiting or severe abdominal pain alongside other allergic symptoms
- Any reaction that is rapidly worsening within minutes

## Plain-language guidance
Remove the person from further exposure to the suspected trigger (stop the food, remove the stinger, etc.) and keep them calm; mild, localized itching or hives without breathing difficulty may be watched closely, but any swelling of the face/throat or breathing trouble is a life-threatening emergency requiring immediate transport to the nearest hospital. A doctor should review the full picture afterward to identify the trigger and plan future prevention.
