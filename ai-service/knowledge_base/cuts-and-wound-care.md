# Cuts and Wound Care

## Common symptoms
- Break in the skin with or without bleeding
- Pain or tenderness around the wound
- Mild swelling or redness at the edges (normal early response)
- Later infection signs: increasing redness, warmth, pus, or foul smell

## Ask the patient about
- How and when did the cut happen, and what caused it (clean object, rusty metal, dirty surface)?
- How deep is the wound, and is bleeding controlled or ongoing?
- Has the wound been cleaned already, and with what?
- Is there any numbness, or is movement affected near the cut (e.g., a finger)?
- Any fever, spreading redness, or pus developing since the injury?
- Is the person's tetanus vaccination up to date?

## Red flags — seek urgent/emergency care immediately
- Bleeding that won't stop with firm, direct pressure
- Deep wounds exposing muscle, tendon, or bone
- Wounds from dirty, rusty, or contaminated objects, especially without recent tetanus protection
- Numbness or inability to move a body part near the cut
- Spreading redness, warmth, pus, fever, or worsening pain (signs of infection)
- Large or gaping wounds that may need stitches

## Plain-language guidance
Clean a fresh cut with clean water and mild soap, apply firm pressure with a clean cloth to stop bleeding, and cover with a clean bandage, changing it regularly and watching for signs of infection. Deeper wounds, wounds from dirty objects, or any that won't stop bleeding should be seen at a clinic for proper cleaning, possible stitches, and tetanus protection. A doctor should review the full picture for anything beyond a small, shallow cut.
