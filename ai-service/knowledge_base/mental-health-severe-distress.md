# Mental Health / Severe Distress Warning Signs

## Common symptoms
- Persistent sadness, hopelessness, or loss of interest in usual activities
- Excessive worry, fear, or anxiety
- Changes in sleep or appetite
- Withdrawal from family, friends, or normal routines
- Difficulty concentrating or making decisions
- Unexplained physical complaints (fatigue, aches) linked to emotional stress

## Ask the patient about
- How long have these feelings or changes been present, and how are they affecting daily life?
- Any changes in sleep, appetite, or energy noticed?
- Any thoughts of self-harm, hopelessness, or feeling like life isn't worth living?
- Is there a support system (family, friends, community) available right now?
- Any recent major stress, loss, or traumatic event?
- Any history of similar episodes, or ongoing treatment for mental health?

## Red flags — seek urgent/emergency care immediately
- Any thoughts, plans, or statements about self-harm or suicide
- Expressions of hopelessness combined with access to means of harm
- Sudden severe agitation, aggression, or inability to stay safe
- Signs of psychosis: hearing voices, seeing things others don't, extreme confusion
- Complete withdrawal or inability to function (not eating, not speaking, not moving)
- Any immediate danger to self or others

## Plain-language guidance
Listen without judgment, stay with the person if they express distress, and remove access to anything that could be used for self-harm while arranging urgent support — this is a medical and safety priority, not something to handle alone. Encourage connection with trusted people and professional help as soon as possible, even if symptoms seem mild at first. A doctor or mental health professional should review the full picture urgently if any safety concern is present.
